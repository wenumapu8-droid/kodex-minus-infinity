## Secciones

### 1. What stretching really is
Gradually expanding a healed lobe so the tissue thickens and adapts, not tears. Done with patience, it stays healthy and reversible to a point; rushed, it scars or blows out. The goal is living tissue, not a number.

### 2. The golden rules (the part that matters most)
- **Only stretch a fully healed piercing.** A fresh lobe is months from ready.
- **One size at a time.** Go up a single gauge, never skip (no 16g → 10g jumps).
- **Wait between stretches.** Minimum 4–6 weeks per size; bigger sizes need longer (months). The lobe tells you when.
- **Never force.** If it hurts, bleeds, or resists — stop. Pain is a no, not a challenge.
- **Tapers are for inserting, not living in.** Insert with a taper + lube, then wear the jewelry.

### 3. Gauge — the map
Lower gauge = thicker. The common path:
20g (0.8mm) · 18g (1.0) · 16g (1.2) · 14g (1.6) · 12g (2.0) · 10g (2.4) · 8g (3.2) · 6g (4) · 4g (5) · 2g (6) · 0g (8) · 00g (10) · then mm direct (11, 12, 14, 16…).

### 4. Materials — what touches the lobe
- **Fresh stretches & sensitive lobes:** implant-grade titanium (ASTM F-136), single-flare with o-rings, or glass. Smooth, inert, easy to clean.
- **Healed, happy lobes only:** organics — wood, stone, bone, horn, our amber/ammonite/fossil pieces. They breathe and feel alive, but never on a fresh stretch or an irritated lobe.
- Avoid: cheap acrylic, externally-threaded or rough jewelry, anything that drags the channel.

### 5. Aftercare — the daily ritual
- **Oil massage** daily with jojoba, vitamin E or emu oil — keeps tissue supple, improves circulation, prevents thinning.
- **Warm sea-salt soaks** if irritated.
- **Listen for thinning:** if the back of the lobe gets thin or tight, you've gone too fast — downsize and wait.
- Keep jewelry and hands clean.

### 6. When it goes wrong
- **Blowout** (a lip of tissue pushed out the back): you stretched too fast. Downsize, massage with oil, wait — often recovers.
- **Tear / bleeding:** stop, remove, let it heal fully, restart slower.
- **The fix is almost always: go down a size and be patient.**
